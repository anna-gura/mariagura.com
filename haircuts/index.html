The appointment

How Your Haircut Works

  1. Consultation and dry-hair diagnosis

    Before your hair gets wet, I look at it the way it actually lives: growth direction, cowlicks around the face, overall density and the thinner and thicker zones, strand thickness, natural wave or curl, and how it falls without styling. We also talk about your routine: how often you style, which tools you use and how much time you want to spend in the morning.

  2. Shape planning

    We agree on the shape and length based on what your hair can actually do. If the photo you brought won't behave the same way on your hair, I explain why and suggest an adapted version.

  3. Wash and preparation

    Depending on your hair type and the technique, I cut on damp hair, dry hair, or a combination of both.

  4. Building the shape

    I section the hair and control elevation, cutting lines, weight distribution and balance. My work draws on Vidal Sassoon geometry, modern Korean cutting techniques and block (panel) cutting methods. I follow the curve of the head to decide where volume should sit and where the shape shouldn't get heavy.

  5. Personalization and check

    I check balance, movement and how the strands fall naturally, so the cut looks right after you wash it at home, not only after a salon blow-dry.

  6. Blow-dry and home-styling tips

    I finish with a professional blow-dry and show you how to dry and style the cut yourself.

Total time: 1.5–2 hours. I book fewer clients a day on purpose; that time goes into adapting the cut to you.

Texture & density

Haircuts for Your Hair Type

Fine or thin hair

The goal is a dense outline so the hair looks fuller. I'm careful with thinning, because removing too much weight makes fine hair look even thinner. A blunt bob, a straight solid line or a bob with minimal graduation usually works best, with layers only where they actually help.

Thick, heavy hair

The goal is to take away the heavy feeling where it bothers you without collapsing the silhouette. Geometric and block techniques let me control where the weight sits.

Wavy hair

I follow the direction of your waves and how the hair behaves once dry, then choose length and layer placement so the waves form on their own.

Curly hair

I don't treat curly hair as straight hair that just needs a blow-dryer. I account for how much the curl shrinks as it dries, density and thickness, different curl patterns in different zones of the head, natural volume, the shape in its dry state, and your daily routine.

Frizz and South Florida humidity

Humidity here changes volume and brings out extra wave. I choose a shape that works with your natural texture, so it doesn't depend on a perfect salon blow-dry, and I'll tell you honestly what the cut can fix and what needs home care and styling products.

Keeping length

Growing Your Hair Out

If you say "I'm growing it out, just a trim," I keep as much length as possible. There's no rule that everyone needs a set number of inches off. If your ends are in good shape, a minimal correction is enough. If there's heavy damage or breakage, I explain why more length needs to go before I cut.

While you grow it out, I keep the layers connected and the outline clean, so it looks good at every in-between stage.

Before I cut

When I'll Suggest a Different Cut

Not every haircut from a photo will look the same on your hair, and I'd rather say so before I cut. Typical situations:

Fine hair and lots of short layers

Many short layers can make the ends of fine hair look sparse. I'll suggest a shape with a solid outline and softer layering.

Short bangs and a strong cowlick

If the growth direction won't let short bangs lie flat on their own, you'd have to style them every day. A longer fringe, curtain bangs or an asymmetric shape that follows the cowlick usually works better.

Curly hair and a photo of a sleek, straight geometric cut

Most of those photos are blown out straight. I adapt the geometry to your real texture and account for curl shrinkage.

Corrections

Fixing a Haircut From Another Salon

Clients often come to me after a cut that didn't work:

  • an uneven bob line or an unbalanced shape
  • top layers cut too short
  • harsh jumps between lengths
  • over-thinned hair and ends that lost density
  • bangs that don't suit the face or won't lie flat
  • a cut that needs complicated styling every day

I start with a diagnosis: what can be fixed in one visit and what has to grow first. Balance, the outline and transitions between lengths can often be corrected right away. Length and density that were cut off can't be restored instantly, so in those cases I give you a step-by-step correction plan over several visits.
